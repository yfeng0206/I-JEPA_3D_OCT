feat(analysis): I-JEPA does not separate inner retina from choroid

Tests the assumption L_rel rests on -- that MIRAGE and I-JEPA should agree
about patch similarity -- decomposed BY TISSUE CLASS using GOALS labels.
30 held-out images, 16x16 grid, cells >=70%% pure (120 inner, 174 choroid).

Mean cosine similarity by class pair:

  model                  in-in   ch-ch   in-ch   contrast
  MIRAGE H0              0.942   0.935  -0.523    +1.461
  MIRAGE enc             0.664   0.669   0.304    +0.362
  JEPA ep100             0.837   0.785   0.719    +0.092
  JEPA ep30              0.897   0.866   0.803    +0.079
  JEPA untrained         0.696   0.744   0.526    +0.194

MIRAGE holds the classes apart (anti-correlated at H0). I-JEPA does not: its
cross-class similarity is nearly as high as within-class, so an inner-retina
patch and a choroid patch look almost the same.

I-JEPA is WORSE than an untrained network at this (+0.079/+0.092 vs +0.194).
Pretraining halved the relational separation of the two tissues. What it did
learn is tissue-vs-background (+0.435 untrained -> +0.517 trained) -- exactly
what predicting masked regions rewards.

The damage mechanism observed directly: applying the encoder adapter pulls
MIRAGE toward I-JEPA's geometry. inner-chor 0.304 -> 0.356 (a=0.05) -> 0.672
(a=0.50), i.e. 83%% of the way to I-JEPA's 0.719, with contrast collapsing 55%%
and Dice falling 0.9457 -> 0.9328. The Dice loss IS this collapse.

Corroborating: UNTRAINED JEPA agrees more with MIRAGE's block structure
(r=0.738) than either trained one (r=0.663).

Implication: no placement/width/depth/lr can fix this, because the target
itself lacks the information. Explains the monotone alpha damage, the
neutral-at-best ceiling, and why the 11x more efficient encoder tap still
cannot help.

(a=0.05 visually identical to frozen; a=0.50 visibly blurs the boundary).
