feat(analysis): scale-free separation metrics for class relations

Block means are not comparable across models -- each has its own similarity
scale, so a model with globally higher cosine values appears to have stronger
within-class similarity without separating anything. Adds Cohen's d and a
discrimination AUC: P(random same-tissue pair more similar than random
different-tissue pair), where 0.5 is chance.

  model              within  between  Cohen d  discrim AUC
  MIRAGE H0           0.937   -0.523    20.20       1.0000
  MIRAGE enc          0.667    0.304     2.82       0.9773
  JEPA ep100          0.800    0.718     0.65       0.6945
  JEPA ep30           0.875    0.803     0.84       0.7714
  JEPA untrained      0.730    0.526     1.38       0.8288

MIRAGE at H0 never confuses the two tissues (AUC 1.0000); I-JEPA is barely
above chance (0.6945). Effect size gap 4.3x.

Critically this shows the absolute numbers mislead: I-JEPA's within-class
similarity (0.800) is HIGHER than MIRAGE's (0.667), but 0.800 vs 0.718 is
almost no gap while 0.667 vs 0.304 is a large one.

The untrained control beats both trained models on every scale-free measure,
confirming the collapse is caused by pretraining, not by scale.
