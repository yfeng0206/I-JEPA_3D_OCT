Anatomy-Guided Target Selection for I-JEPA}

\begin{abstract}
Learning transferable representations from retinal optical coherence
tomography (OCT) is challenging because disease-related changes can be subtle
and large, consistently labelled datasets require substantial expert
annotation. Self-supervised learning offers a way to use unlabelled scans
and reduce dependence on diagnostic labels. Image-based Joint-Embedding
Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA) learns by predicting target-region
representations from visible context rather than reconstructing pixels.
However, its uniform target placement does not account for the layered
organization of retinal OCT, where retinal tissue occupies only part of
each B-scan. We investigate anatomy-guided target selection using
image intensity and predicted retinal segmentation while retaining the
I-JEPA architecture and latent prediction objective.
We compare tissue-directed rectangular targets, anatomy-shaped targets
and coverage-constrained placement with uniform masking, and evaluate
the learned representations through frozen-encoder glaucoma classification
on FairVision. Tissue-directed rectangle placement improves the observed
classification results, including with segmentation-free intensity guidance,
while more anatomically specific strategies do not consistently provide
further gains. Analysis of the actual masks shows that target selection,
target size and visible context are coupled. Our findings motivate
anatomy-guided predictive learning for retinal OCT and better-controlled
comparisons of what to predict and what to keep visible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) reveals the layered structure of the retina
and supports glaucoma assessment~\citep{maetschke2019glaucoma,ran2019glaucoma,noury2022glaucoma}.
Self-supervised pretraining supports medical imaging foundation models by
learning reusable representations without requiring a diagnosis for every
training scan~\citep{azizi2021big,zhou2023retfound,qiu2024visionfm}.
The spatial organization of OCT makes the choice of pretraining task
particularly relevant: retinal tissue forms a relatively narrow region within
a B-scan, surrounded by vitreous and other background.

Image-based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture
(I-JEPA)~\citep{assran2023ijepa} learns by predicting hidden image
representations rather than reconstructing hidden pixels. A context encoder
processes visible patches. A predictor uses these features and the target
positions to estimate representations supplied by a target encoder.
The target encoder is updated by an exponential moving average (EMA) of
the context encoder. This creates a prediction task without diagnosis labels.
Unlike masked autoencoders~\citep{he2022mae,xie2022simmim}, I-JEPA does not
require the predictor to reproduce each pixel.

Our interest in anatomical guidance also arose from an exploratory
feature-occlusion analysis of glaucoma classifiers initialized from
uniform-masking I-JEPA and subsequently fine-tuned with diagnosis labels.
Selected attribution maps showed prediction sensitivity near retinal
structures: removing patch features from the pooled representation changed
the classifier logit. This observation motivated asking whether retinal
anatomy could guide target selection during pretraining
(Appendix~\ref{app:interp}).

Standard I-JEPA places rectangular targets uniformly across the image.
In retinal OCT, many such targets include little retinal tissue. This raises
our central question: \emph{can I-JEPA benefit from selecting prediction
targets according to retinal anatomy rather than uniformly across the image?}
Figure~\ref{fig:target_selection} illustrates the change in target placement.
Semantic and anatomy-guided masked modelling provide positive motivation
for this question~\citep{li2022semmae,kakogeorgiou2022attmask,li2024anatomask,wang2025maskwhatmatters}.
However, selecting a target also removes information from the context.
The useful learning task depends on both what is predicted and what remains
visible.

We investigate this question through five masking families, including
segmentation-free intensity guidance and retinal segmentation from
MIRAGE~\citep{morano2025mirage}. These strategies vary target location,
shape, or anatomical coverage while retaining the I-JEPA architecture and
latent prediction objective. We evaluate the resulting B-scan representations
by pooling them across an OCT volume and fitting a linear glaucoma classifier.
The study includes six implementations, with two versions of anatomy-shaped
masking.

Tissue-directed rectangle placement gives higher AUC than uniform masking in
the reported runs. More detailed anatomical targeting does not consistently
improve on these simpler strategies. To understand what the comparisons test,
we measure the target and context patches that actually reach the model.
This analysis identifies differences in target size, retained context and
repeated target indices, as well as a coverage-scoring mismatch. We therefore
present comparisons of implemented masking strategies, not controlled
ablations of anatomical coverage.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item An empirical comparison of intensity-guided, segmentation-guided and
      uniform target selection for I-JEPA on retinal OCT.
\item Frozen-probe results showing gains from tissue-directed rectangle
      placement, alongside mixed results for more specific anatomical targeting.
\item An analysis of the actual target and context masks that identifies
      implementation constraints for better-controlled guided-masking studies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Masked image modelling and JEPAs.}
Masked autoencoders reconstruct pixels~\citep{he2022mae,xie2022simmim},
whereas BEiT predicts discrete visual tokens~\citep{bao2022beit} and iBOT
distils teacher outputs at masked patch positions~\citep{zhou2022ibot}.
Data2vec and I-JEPA learn by predicting latent
representations~\citep{baevski2022data2vec,assran2023ijepa}.
Related approaches extend masked prediction to video and volumetric
data~\citep{bardes2024vjepa,chen2023mim3d,taleb20203dssl}.
JEPA formulations also differ in their objectives and prediction
structure~\citep{balestriero2025lejepa,he2025dseqjepa}; we retain the
I-JEPA objective and study target selection.

\paragraph{Region selection and prediction structure.}
DSeq-JEPA combines attention-derived region selection with sequential
prediction~\citep{he2025dseqjepa}. Its highest-ranked region supplies
context for predicting subsequent regions. Its component ablation favors
the joint design rather than region selection alone. Our study instead uses
retinal anatomy and parallel I-JEPA prediction. This distinction matters:
a guidance map, the selected targets and the available context jointly
define the learning task. I-JEPA's own masking analysis emphasizes both
sufficiently large targets and informative context~\citep{assran2023ijepa}.

\paragraph{Medical region-conditioned prediction.}
US-JEPA uses a frozen ultrasound teacher, and its region-conditioned variant
restricts both context and target sampling to valid ultrasound
content~\citep{radhachandran2026usjepa}. Our study addresses a different
question: how intensity-based and retinal-segmentation guidance change
target selection within the EMA-based I-JEPA framework for OCT.
We compare simple rectangle placement with anatomy-shaped and
coverage-constrained targets, without introducing sequential prediction
or a new teacher architecture. The contribution is this OCT comparison
and analysis of the resulting prediction tasks, not the general idea
of informed masking.

\paragraph{Informed masking.}
ADIOS learns adversarial masks~\citep{shi2022adios}; SemMAE uses semantic
parts~\citep{li2022semmae}; AttMask selects high-attention
patches~\citep{kakogeorgiou2022attmask}; and HardPatches and AutoMAE adapt
masking to prediction difficulty~\citep{wang2023hpm,chen2023automae}.
Medical approaches include AnatoMask, AnatomyMAE, MSMAE and
VasoMIM~\citep{li2024anatomask,ceballosarroyo2026anatomymae,mao2023msmae,huang2026vasomim}.
These studies motivate informed masking, but their objectives, guidance
sources and context construction differ. SemMAE, for example, gradually
changes from within-part to whole-part prediction rather than masking whole
parts from the start. We study anatomy-guided target selection under latent
prediction on retinal OCT.

\paragraph{Retinal foundation models and OCT.}
RETFound~\citep{zhou2023retfound}, VisionFM~\citep{qiu2024visionfm},
URFound~\citep{yu2024urfound}, and OCTCube~\citep{liu2026octcube} pretrain on
large retinal corpora. OCT-specific masked modelling is explored by
\citet{pissas2024octmim}. MIRAGE~\citep{morano2025mirage} provides multimodal
OCT segmentation and supplies the anatomical guidance used by several of our
arms, and deep glaucoma detection from OCT is
established~\citep{maetschke2019glaucoma,ran2019glaucoma,noury2022glaucoma}.
FairVision provides the glaucoma-labelled OCT data used for our
downstream comparison~\citep{luo2024fairvision}.

\section{Background and target-selection framework}
\label{sec:policies}

We first define the I-JEPA prediction task, then describe how each masking
strategy selects its targets. The architecture and latent prediction loss
are shared; differences in guide sources, target size and final context
are part of the strategy comparisons.

\subsection{I-JEPA preliminaries}

Let $x$ be a B-scan partitioned into $N$ patches, and let $C$ denote the
indices of the patches supplied to the context encoder. Target group $T_m$
contains $K_m$ patch indices, with $m=1,\ldots,M$. We treat $T_m$ as an
index list because some implementations repeat indices when equalizing target
lengths. The context excludes the union of target positions.
Writing $p_C$ and $p_{T_m}$ for the corresponding positional embeddings,
the context and prediction paths are
\begin{equation}
 h_C=f_\theta(x;C), \qquad
 \widehat z_m=g_\phi(h_C,p_C,p_{T_m}).
 \label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}
Here $f_\theta(x;C)$ embeds only the selected patches at their original image
positions. The predictor projects $h_C$ to its own feature dimension and
adds its own positional embeddings $p_C$; target queries combine a learned
mask token with $p_{T_m}$. The target encoder sees the full B-scan. Its output is normalized
per patch before selecting the target positions:
\begin{equation}
 z_m=\operatorname{sg}\!\left(
       [\operatorname{LN}(f_{\bar\theta}(x))]_{T_m}
      \right), \qquad
 \bar\theta\leftarrow \mu\bar\theta+(1-\mu)\theta .
 \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
The operator $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients, $\operatorname{LN}$ is
LayerNorm across feature dimensions, and $\mu$ is the EMA coefficient.
The predictor uses the same context for each target group; predictions are
not conditioned on earlier target predictions.

Each target and prediction is a $K_m\times D$ matrix.
For feature dimension $D$, the implementation minimizes the mean Smooth-L1
loss over target instances and feature coordinates:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L =
 \frac{1}{D\sum_{m=1}^{M}K_m}
 \sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{K_m}\sum_{d=1}^{D}
 \rho\!\left(\widehat z_{m,k,d}-z_{m,k,d}\right).
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The Smooth-L1 function $\rho$ uses the standard unit transition between its
quadratic and linear regions. Repeated indices contribute repeated loss
terms. The code equalizes target lengths within a batch and applies a
single mean reduction to the stacked prediction and target tensors,
including averaging over images.
Figure~\ref{fig:policies} shows the pretraining and evaluation paths.

\subsection{Anatomy-guided target selection}

For strategy $s$, let $A_s(x)$ denote its patch-grid guidance:
an intensity-derived band for \ArmBest{}, a repaired segmentation envelope
for \textsc{envelope}, or soft retinal-class score maps for the anatomy-shaped
and coverage strategies. These signals need not have the same channel
structure. \ArmBest{} is an intensity-guided tissue-localization baseline,
not a segmentation model. We write the joint masking procedure as
\begin{equation}
 (C,T)\sim\Pi_s(\,\cdot\mid A_s(x)),
 \qquad T=(T_1,\ldots,T_M).
 \label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
The random baseline uses geometric block sampling without consulting
image guidance. $\Pi_s$ includes target placement and context construction,
including batch-dependent token equalization.
A candidate context block is sampled, target positions are
excluded, and token lists are shortened as needed for batching. Only the
remaining context tokens enter $f_\theta$. Consequently, tissue outside the
target union need not be visible to the encoder.
We define \emph{mask ratio} as the number of unique target positions divided
by $N$, and \emph{tissue coverage} as the fraction of guide-positive patches
included in that union. Neither is the fraction of the image supplied as
context.

\subsection{Compared masking strategies}

All policies request $M{=}4$ target blocks and one context block. Rectangle
policies share nominal scale and aspect-ratio ranges, but placement and
collation can change unique target area and encoder-visible context.

\begin{description}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[\textsc{random} (baseline).] Stock I-JEPA multiblock. Four rectangles placed
  uniformly at random anywhere in the grid. No image content is consulted.

\item[\textsc{envelope} (random-within-retina).] The same nominal rectangle
count, scale and aspect-ratio distributions as \textsc{random}, but
rejection-sampled onto the MIRAGE-derived repaired retinal
envelope~\citep{morano2025mirage}. The predicted inner-retina and choroid union
is repaired across the unlabeled middle-retina gap in native label space,
then transformed jointly with the image. This
  changes the placement policy, not target shape. Overlap and delivered context
  are consequences of that intervention, not independently matched controls.

\item[\ArmBest{} (segmentation-free).] A band whose vertical position is
  located per slice by a per-column intensity-weighted row centroid, smoothed
  across columns, with no segmentation model and no ground-truth
  annotation of any kind. This band guides the placement of four rectangular
  targets; it is not itself the target shape.

\item[\textsc{anatomy-v1} / \textsc{anatomy-v2}.] Ragged connected components
  grown on the MIRAGE score map, so target \emph{shape} follows tissue rather
  than being rectangular. v2 also bridges diagonal adjacencies.

\item[\textsc{cover} $f$.] Targets chosen greedily to cover anatomy while
  leaving a fraction $f$ of supported soft guide mass outside the target union. The historical run uses
  $f{=}0.21$. This condition does not guarantee the same fraction survives
  final encoder-context collation, and post-placement target truncation changes
  the intended coverage (Appendix~\ref{app:covbug}).
\end{description}

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use FairVision OCT volumes with binary glaucoma
labels~\citep{luo2024fairvision} and the fixed released splits.
Training and validation contain 6{,}000 and 1{,}000 volumes, respectively.
Each volume contributes 100 B-scans, resized to $256{\times}256$.
The test split contains $N{=}\Ntest$ volumes (\Npos{} positive).
Diagnosis labels are used only for downstream evaluation, not JEPA pretraining.
Demographic attributes are not model inputs.

\paragraph{Pretraining.} Patch-level I-JEPA, ViT-Base, $256{\times}256$ crops,
patch size 16, predictor depth 6, EMA target encoder. The reference fork is the
epoch-25 checkpoint, with matched nominal optimiser, learning-rate and
weight-decay schedules and effective batch size (512).
Guidance is ramped linearly from epoch 25 to 30 and held at full strength
thereafter. The early \textsc{anatomy-v1} result has incomplete launch
documentation; its configuration resumes an \textsc{envelope} epoch-27
checkpoint rather than directly documenting an epoch-25 fork.
The encoder processes B-scans independently, without learned cross-slice
attention. Rectangle strategies share nominal target scales, but the guide
products and realized masks are not identical. \textsc{anatomy-v2} also uses
fixed-length targets. These differences are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\textsc{random}, \ArmBest{}, \textsc{envelope} and \textsc{cover} reach
epoch 100. \textsc{anatomy-v2} reaches epoch 75; its epoch-100 result is
unmeasured. \textsc{anatomy-v1} has only an epoch-30 result. We compare
strategies at matched pretraining epochs, with epoch 50 as the common
endpoint for the five main policies. Excluded continuations are listed
separately in Appendix~\ref{app:excluded}.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} The EMA target encoder is frozen. We average patch features within each
B-scan, then average across the volume and train a LayerNorm--linear head.
Each head uses a 50-epoch budget and is selected by validation AUC.
We report test AUC with percentile bootstrap intervals and paired differences
(\Nboot{} class-stratified resamples, fitted heads fixed), together with
DeLong tests~\citep{delong1988roc}. Paired analyses rely on the documented
shared split and deterministic loader ordering; historical prediction files
lack independent case-ID manifests. These intervals describe test-case
sampling, not variation across pretraining runs.

\paragraph{Numerical precision.} The original rectangle-policy probes use mixed precision, while the
anatomy-shaped and coverage-policy probes use fp32. All primary differences
in Table~\ref{tab:main} use a baseline matched in both epoch and probe
precision. Re-probes quantify the small numerical differences
(Appendix~\ref{app:fp32}); probe precision is distinct from pretraining
target-encoder precision.

some policies change several aspects of the prediction task.

\paragraph{Anatomy-shaped targets.}
At the matched epoch 50, \textsc{anatomy-v2}
has lower AUC than \textsc{envelope} ($\Delta=\DAnatomyTwoEnvelopeEpFifty$; CI

\paragraph{Coverage-constrained placement.}
\textsc{cover} improves to epoch 50, peaks at epoch
$\CoverPeakEpoch$, then \emph{falls}, losing $\DCoverPeakToHundred$ from its own
peak ($p\,\DCoverPeakToHundredP$) while the unguided null keeps improving
(Figure~\ref{fig:traj}).
Although \textsc{cover} selects rectangles by anatomical coverage, batching
shortens the targets after placement. Its final masks therefore hide less
anatomy than \textsc{envelope}'s (Table~\ref{tab:geom}), rather than providing
a higher-coverage comparison. Appendix~\ref{app:covbug} describes this
target-sizing behavior.

\ArmBest{} has the highest epoch-100 point estimate, exceeding
\textsc{envelope} by $\DOracleEnvelopeEpHundred$ (CI
$\DOracleEnvelopeEpHundredCI$). The paired \ArmBest{}--\textsc{envelope}
difference intervals include zero at epochs 50 and 75.
Thus a simple intensity-based strategy is a useful comparator
for segmentation-guided methods, not an anatomical oracle.

The samplers do not differ only in how much tissue they target.
\textsc{anatomy-v2} has high tissue purity but a smaller target union and
more total context than the rectangle strategies (Table~\ref{tab:geom}).
Its fixed $K{=}16$ indices per target give $4\times16=64$ prediction
instances, compared with about $159$ for the rectangle strategies.
This is an explicit target-length choice, not an inherent property of
irregular shapes. Repeated target indices also affect loss weighting.
The anatomy-shaped and coverage strategies use different guide products
from the historical \textsc{envelope} run (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

We therefore report target and context geometry alongside AUC.
Appendix~\ref{app:geomprov} gives the per-image measurement protocol;
Appendix~\ref{app:delivered_audit} measures the final masks in complete
training-sized batches. Together, these distinguish tissue outside the
target union from tissue actually supplied to the context encoder.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The positive rectangle-policy results motivate anatomy-guided target
selection without requiring a new encoder architecture. The segmentation-free
centroid strategy is particularly useful as a simple baseline.
More specific strategies also changed target size, guide source and
retained context. Future comparisons should control these factors while
varying the target-selection rule.

\paragraph{Experimental limits.}
Each policy has one pretraining continuation, so test-case confidence
intervals do not establish a ranking over retrainings~\citep{bouthillier2021variance}.
The early anatomy-shaped result has incomplete continuation documentation,
and \textsc{anatomy-v2} was stopped after inspecting its epoch-75 deficit.
Its later trajectory is unknown. Probe optimization and initialization
can also affect frozen-probe comparisons; the reported fixed-budget
protocol is not a measurement of the best achievable classifier for each
encoder. Repeated pretraining comparisons remain future work.

The study uses one dataset and one downstream task. Policies, checkpoints
and analyses were chosen after repeated inspection of the same test split,
although probe heads were fitted on training data and selected on validation
data. Reported intervals are therefore descriptive rather than confirmatory.
Segmentation accuracy on the OCT pretraining data is not independently
established. Only the specified coverage setting was pretrained, so we
identify neither an optimal coverage level nor a mechanism for its AUC decline.

\paragraph{Broader impact and ethics.}
This study uses a public, de-identified retinal dataset under its release terms
and involves no new recruitment or data collection, and no model reported here
is clinically validated or intended for deployment (Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}).

\section{Conclusion}

We investigated where I-JEPA should predict in retinal OCT.
Tissue-directed rectangle placement improves frozen-probe glaucoma AUC in
the observed runs, including a simple intensity-centroid strategy.
More anatomically specific targeting does not consistently improve these
results, and the implementations differ in more than target location.
The next step is to compare target selection under matched guidance,
target sizes and visible context across repeated pretraining runs.

\section{Target sizing and visible context}
\label{app:covbug}

\paragraph{Batch equalization.}
Predictor targets are shortened to the minimum target length in the
microbatch. \textsc{cover} chooses placements using the anatomical mass
inside full candidate rectangles before this equalization.
Because target indices are ordered row-major, shortening removes the
lower portion of each rectangle rather than a spatially random subset.
Coverage at placement time can consequently differ from coverage at the loss.

\paragraph{Measured geometry.}
The retained floor sweep over $6{,}137$ slices gives anatomical coverage of
$73.1\%$ for \textsc{cover} and $77.6\%$ for \textsc{envelope}.
Across $24{,}000$ emitted targets, $73.4\%$ remain perfect rectangles.
Thus the trained \textsc{cover} policy is not a higher-coverage counterpart
to \textsc{envelope}. Table~\ref{tab:geom} reports the final target sets,
and Appendix~\ref{app:delivered_audit} provides a fixed-crop batch replay.

\textsc{envelope} shares the same shortening rule but does not optimize an
exact coverage level. The anatomy-shaped strategies instead resample
targets to a fixed $K{=}16$ indices. Equalization therefore has different
consequences across the strategies, even when nominal target counts agree.

\section{Final target and context masks}

\paragraph{Scope.}
We replayed the actual guided dataset, paired transforms, DataLoader worker
collator and returned mask tensors on the existing 600-view Training scope
(24 volumes, 25 views each), using fixed new crop draws shared across policies.
These are not reconstructed historical training crops. Drawn block sizes were
injected independently of placement; equal seeds alone do not pair

the policy's soft-mass constraint on the target complement.

\paragraph{Random fill is not a background quota.}
At full ramp all usable targets take the guided branch, while the
\texttt{random\_legal} fill mechanism is conditional on unused target blocks.

\section{Motivating attribution analysis and evidence limits}
\label{app:interp}

\paragraph{Origin of the target-selection question.}
Before studying anatomy-guided masking, we inspected feature-occlusion maps
from glaucoma classifiers initialized with the uniformly masked I-JEPA
baseline and then fine-tuned. The analysis compared mean pooling,
cross-attention pooling and an attentive probe.
At the B-scan level, a slice representation was zeroed before re-evaluating
the classifier. At the patch level, an encoded patch was omitted from its
B-scan's patch mean, the mean was recomputed from the remaining patches,
and the altered slice representation was passed to the pooling module
and classification head. The change in logit measures sensitivity to this
feature intervention; it is not pixel occlusion or an attention map.

Let $F$ denote the sequence of B-scan representations supplied to the
classifier and $\ell(F)$ its glaucoma logit. If $F^{(-s,p)}$ replaces
the representation of B-scan $s$ with the mean of its remaining patch
features after omitting patch $p$, the attribution is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{s,p}=\ell(F)-\ell\!\left(F^{(-s,p)}\right).
 \label{eq:occlusion_delta}
\end{equation}
Positive values mean that removing the feature lowers the glaucoma logit;
negative values mean that removal raises it. Both signs indicate
prediction sensitivity. Figure~\ref{fig:archived_occlusion} shows selected
archived examples of the resulting spatial patterns.

Each pair shows an OCT B-scan and its patch-attribution overlay.
Red denotes positive $\Delta_{s,p}$ and blue negative $\Delta_{s,p}$

\end{figure}

The historical report and selected overlays prompted us to investigate retinal
regions as prediction targets. Those examples were selected from correctly
classified cases and informative slices, so they are not a population-level
estimate of anatomical localization. Nor does a response to feature removal
establish a clinically causal region or show which pretraining mask is best.
Fine-tuning used diagnosis labels, so the maps cannot separate the contribution
of self-supervised pretraining from subsequent supervised adaptation.

\paragraph{Available evidence.}
Archived occlusion curves and heat maps remain available, but the underlying
per-volume numerical arrays and a complete normalization record are not
retained in the evidence used for this submission. The historical record also
documents sensitivity of patch-level displays to numerical precision and
colour scaling. The selected image panels are retained as archived
visualizations, without restoring their numerical summaries as validated
results. We make no spatial-consistency, laterality or clinical
landmark claim from those displays. Recovering the producing arrays,
checkpoint identities and plotting transforms would be necessary for a
reproducible quantitative analysis. No new attribution campaign was run,
and the saved frozen-probe AUC comparisons are unchanged.
